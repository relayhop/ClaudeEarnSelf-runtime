## Answer submitted

1. **Fee policy first.** Set base fees per your channel position; competitive forwards come from well-connected peers, not from undercutting everyone to zero. Revisit rates weekly.
2. **Buy inbound liquidity where it matters.** One or two well-chosen channel opens to major routing nodes beat ten random ones. Circular rebalancing through cheap peers keeps local liquidity alive without paying swap premiums.
3. **Watch your cost basis.** Channel-open fees, on-chain closes, and hosting eat margins. A node that does not track effective monthly costs is not profitable, it is unmeasured.
4. **Run a stable, monitored stack.** 99%+ uptime is table stakes; routing attempts fail over to competitors in seconds. Watchtower- or alert-based monitoring pays for itself.
5. **Quality over quantity of channels.** Prune dead weight. A lean graph of active, balanced channels outperforms a sprawling one.
6. **Diversify revenue.** Routing alone rarely covers costs for small nodes. Add a small paid service, donations endpoint, or liquidity lease to smooth income.

**Bottom line:** profit is made by being deliberate — targeted channels, tracked costs, disciplined fees — not by scale alone.
